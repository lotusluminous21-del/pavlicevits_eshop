## 0. The translation doctrine

The brand owner's personal voice — visible on his Instagram saved/curated reel grid — is **intense, disciplined, lone-wolf, wounded-but-healed**. It carries strong values (discipline, gratitude, generosity, refusal to compromise) wrapped in a register that's intimate, sometimes adversarial, and emotionally raw.

The brand voice is the **public professional version of the same person**. Same backbone. Different register. The translation principle:

| Personal register (IG) | Public register (brand) |
|---|---|
| "I owe it to myself" | "We hold ourselves to a standard that doesn't bend." |
| "I'm working to disappear" | "We don't compete for attention. We compete for the work." |
| "Mama raised a soldier" | "Our standards were taught by people who held them before us." |
| "Focus on improving, not proving" | "We're not interested in being seen. We're interested in being right." |
| "We rise by lifting others" | "When our customers' projects last, our reputation lasts." |
| "Money is a tool, not the goal. The goal is freedom" | "Our measure of success is repeat customers and long-term relationships." |
| "Humble enough to be replaced. Wise enough to know there's no one like me" | "We don't claim to be the only choice. We work to be a worthy one." |
| "Just because we prefer peace doesn't mean we're not ready for war" | "Quiet shop. Ready hands." |
| "We mature by damage and not by age" | "Three decades of doing the work. Each one taught us something the last didn't." |
| "If your dreams don't scare you, they're not big enough" | "We take on the projects most shops won't quote for." |

**What stays**: discipline, standards, gratitude, generosity, family/legacy continuity, forward momentum, substance-over-performance, long-term thinking, refusal to compromise on quality.

**What's removed**: self-pity, adversarial language, lone-wolf glorification, overt masculinity tropes, contradictions, anything wounded, anything "I" — replaced with "we."

The voice is **calm, confident, specific, generous, forward-looking**. It speaks to peers, not to followers. It builds trust through quiet competence, not through declarations.

---

## 1. Universal voice rules (carried across all pages)

1. **"We," never "I."** The brand is the family + the team + the legacy. Singular voice flattens that.
2. **Specifics beat superlatives.** "Seven-year service life" beats "exceptional durability."
3. **The customer is the actor, we are the partner.** Not "We give you the best paint." But "Bring us the job. We'll match the system to it."
4. **Forward-looking verbs.** "Will hold up," "comes back to us," "lasts" — not "has been," "was."
5. **No defensive language.** Never explain what we're not. Only show what we are.
6. **No combat metaphors.** No "war," "battle," "fight," "soldier." The discipline is real; the metaphors are off-brand.
7. **Acknowledge the customer's expertise.** They came to us for a reason. Don't lecture. Confirm and add.
8. **End with invitation, never with sale.** "Talk to us" / "Bring us the job" — not "Buy now" / "Get a quote today."

### 6.4 Curation principle

**Eyebrow chip**:
> HOW WE CHOOSE

**Mixed-weight headline**:
> **Six tests.**
> ***Every product passes them, or it isn't on our shelves.***

**Body**:
> When we evaluate whether a product earns shelf space, we apply six tests in this order:

1. **Performance under real conditions** — not lab spec sheets. We use it ourselves or watch trusted painters use it.
2. **Pigment integrity over time** — does the color hold at year five, or fade by year two?
3. **Coverage and yield** — does the listed yield match field reality?
4. **Substrate compatibility** — does it adhere properly across the substrates our customers actually face?
5. **Documented technical support** — datasheets, MSDS, manufacturer responsiveness when there's a question.
6. **Safety and certification** — VOC compliance, eco-certifications where claimed.

> If a product fails any of these in our experience, it leaves the shelf.

## 13. Voice usage notes for the design crew

When laying out the copy, watch for these moments where the typography and copy must work together:

### Mixed-weight headlines (the signature device)

Every primary headline uses the heavy + light italic pattern. The italic phrase is **always** the modifier or the second beat — never the opener. The italic carries the editorial rhythm.

Examples:
- Heavy: "The right paint." Italic: "*For work that has to last.*"
- Heavy: "What's in the can" Italic: "*is the actual product.*"
- Heavy: "Six services." Italic: "*Each grounded in real work.*"

### The slogan

Always italic. Always English. Always brand color (petrol). Always sub-line position when paired with logo.

### Eyebrow chips

Always UPPERCASE. Always small (12px / body/xs). Always +0.06em letter-spacing. Used to label sections.

### Quote treatment

Pull-quotes get the editorial-quote organism treatment — display/sm italic, brand color, with em-dash attribution below in body/sm secondary.

### Numbers

Tabular numerals for stats, contact info, tech grids. Lining numerals for body.

## 14. Greek translation guidance (for when the site goes to Greek)

The website will likely be served primarily in Greek. The English copy in this document serves three purposes:

1. **The brand voice template** for the Greek translator to follow.
2. **The international visitor experience** for marina chandlers, foreign hotel project managers, etc.
3. **The slogan and brand vocabulary** which stays in English regardless ("Making Your Life Colorful," "Pavlicevits Colors").

When the Greek translator works, they should:

- **Translate the meaning**, not the words. The voice rules in Section 1 still apply: "we" not "I," specifics over superlatives, no defensive language, no combat metaphors.
- **Keep the slogan in English** in Greek copy. It stays as "Making Your Life Colorful."
- **Honor the typographic line break of mixed-weight headlines.** The Greek version may need different word order to keep the rhythm. That's fine.
- **Write headers in headline case** for English; **Greek convention** uses sentence case or all-caps for emphasis. Eyebrow chips stay all-caps in both languages.
- **Adjust line lengths** for body copy: Greek tends to run 10-15% longer per line than English equivalent. Container widths are already designed for this.
